# Frozen moving-start recovery

Before any new physics, bind motor 88, the fitted gait, chooser +104, chooser +112, the three-depth parent at 128, the depth fixtures, and all twelve archived +104 traces. Reproduce the accepted substep of each clearance stop, every chooser decision, and the matching whole-body positions. The four starts at each of 72, 75 and 80 mm use their original moving body and phase. Capture the first normal chooser decision at 0.8 seconds after the identical native warm-up.

From that exact state test each of 15 speed/rate requests held for 1, 3, 8 or 16 chooser decisions. Additionally test the strongest turn of each sign at each of three speeds for 4 or 8 decisions, followed by 8 decisions straight or with the opposite rate at the same speed. Give control back to +104 with body, actuator history, clock, and visitation state intact. Compare autonomous +104, +112 and parent policies from the same state. These alternatives only exercise physically available motor requests in the offline assay.

Report elapsed survival, minimum certified whole-body gap (including any rejected sample), mean horizontal COM transport, native maximum stall, and actual speed at the cue. Record whether the branch clears the initial approach by 20 seconds: alive, gaining at least 5 mm above its post-cue minimum, with positive whole-body gap. Full recovery requires reaching 180 seconds from spawn with positive gap throughout, at least 6 mm/s mean COM transport after the standard six-second warm-up, at most 407 consecutive stalled controls, at most 10 degrees tilt and at most 1 mm depth error. One passing branch demonstrates existence for that start. A failed finite search leaves other sequences untested.

Independently translate each original start 60 and 120 mm upstream along its actual initial velocity, retaining native depth, phase and orientation, and log actual starting clearance. Test the three autonomous choosers and both strongest turns at all three speeds for 8 or 16 decisions, releasing to +104. Score using the same 20- and 180-second rules. These extra starts test how much the opportunity to turn changes farther from shore. They cannot earn credit for an original start.

Three independent once-only depth runs, each limited to 180 seconds internally and 185 seconds externally. At most 468 branches per depth (348 original and 120 translated), 67,400,000 attempted physical substeps per depth including the twelve warm-ups and four short baseline traces. Preserve partial output on failure. No optimizer, parameter fitting, scripted deployment, physical changes or resets. A candidate must separately pass the publication protocol.

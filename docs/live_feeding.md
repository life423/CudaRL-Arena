# Feed the tadpole while it learns — October 28, 2026

Choose **Open pond** in the NVIDIA developer build. Click or tap open water to drop a pellet. Food is born just below the surface, sinks, drifts under water drag, and rests or slides at the bed. The tadpole's inherited goal selector chooses its own target and its swimming network produces the four tail-joint commands. No cursor-following, scripted interception or remote food collection is added.

There is no three-pellet replacement rule. Every valid drop adds a pellet without deleting another. A reported **128-active-pellet resource guard** prevents unbounded buildup; excess drops are rejected visibly. Every active pellet is scored by the shared goal network. The motor receives the selected target and nearby context, not a three-food decision shortlist. **Drop food at pond center** is a keyboard-accessible alternative; the focused pond also supports arrow-key placement and Space/Enter. Invalid shoreline drops show feedback.

**Current** retains the steady 0.5/0.75 cm/s choices. Pellets continue sinking when current is off; their horizontal speed responds through drag and inertia rather than teleporting to the new fluid velocity. **Closer view** changes the camera. **Pause learning** pauses PPO while the visible physical simulation continues. **Save training now** requests a full checkpoint. Leaving the pond or closing the app saves and stops; reopening resumes. Learning does not continue after the process exits.

## What genuinely learns

The swimming actor starts from the preserved motor at update 64,350. It expands from 33 to 37 observations: target relative velocity and target presence are added with zero initial input weights, preserving the inherited motor function before training. Native Gaussian PPO then updates the motor and its training value estimator. The inherited progress-aware food selector (update 320) remains frozen in this stage and scores all active foods using symmetric summaries. There are still two decision controllers; the critic is PPO's training helper, not another creature ability.

A concurrent native C++/CUDA worker runs **1,024 practice worlds by default**, paced to at most two PPO updates per second initially. The user’s recent drop locations and imposed current update the practice context at rollout boundaries. Practice samples 1–8 feeding pellets and varied food ages/starts; a quarter of worlds retain the earlier calm/static-food task. These worlds practice user-created conditions rather than replaying the visible trajectory exactly. Visible inference data is not mixed off-policy into PPO. Rendering and native live physics continue alongside training.

The initial frozen measurement is intentionally brief: **eight worlds × ten simulated seconds**, not a lengthy pretrained sinking-food experiment. Every sixteen updates, original/incumbent/candidate actors are compared on separately seeded matched trials. Adoption requires finite/nonstalled trials, no loss of feeding-food count, improvement in food-plus-final-distance score, and no loss of the original actor's calm-food count. This is a small provisional gate, not comprehensive retention or arbitrary-water proof. A validated actor reaches the live creature at a simulation-step boundary without changing its physical state or counters.

The top/graph show actual **PPO update** and **adopted update** separately. They need not advance together. **AI details** shows matched candidate/reference results and interaction/practice versions. The graph itself measures actual session food/minute; adding more food changes that task, so it is not comparable to historical three-food scores and is not proof of learning by itself. Improvements are not guaranteed after each drop or update.

## Saving and preserved references

Feeding uses `user://pond-policies/feeding-learning-v1/training.bin`, a separate checksummed, atomically written, leased checkpoint. It contains actor, critic, log standard deviations, all optimizer moments/counters, practice worlds, context, validated actor/version and the visible body/pellet state. Autosave is every thirty seconds and on graceful exit, including while learning is paused; a crash can lose progress since the last successful save. Chart samples/evaluation history rebuild on reopen; the physical/session counters and model progress resume. Save errors are surfaced in the pond and AI details.

The original policy files and protected earlier full saves are untouched. **Experiments → Trained reference** retains the previous live CPU/CUDA pond and its random-weight comparison. The new CUDA learner does not secretly replace that reference. A machine without working CUDA opens the reference path from the main menu; this first sinking-food learning mode is not a CPU-training/distribution release. The help dialog labels the older headless selector console as a separate preserved task, not a continuation of the new feeding learner.

## Verification and limits

The release extension builds with strict warnings. Four focused native cases cover function-preserving input expansion, sinking/drift/bed contact/capacity, exact three-food feature equivalence, all-candidate CUDA selection beyond slot three, CPU/CUDA physics agreement and correct time-limit bootstrap observations. Nine existing current/reliability/food-drift regressions also pass with GPU required and zero skips. A full native-suite rerun is not claimed.

Subsequent integration verification, requested with commit/push/merge: the complete native suite passes **180/180**, GPU required and zero skips. The preserved portable C++-only runtime test and portable Godot extension build also pass. Logs are archived with the feeding evidence. The live pond stayed open during these correctness checks; their timing is not an isolated performance benchmark. Hosted branch/main CI is verified separately for each exact pushed commit.

The 1,024-world rendered check passes **171 assertions in the recorded run**, including real pointer/tap/keyboard feeding, weight changes, practice-context consumption, two candidate comparisons, no-reset stepping, physical/session restore and four window sizes. At update 16, the matched feeding check improved from **9.833 to 10.333 food/min**, with calm counts **12/12**, and the accepted actor was applied live. The update-32 candidate tied and was rejected; adoption stayed at 16. These are small implementation checks, not a statistically established general improvement. A separate brief check also explicitly resumes optimizer/world state and continues PPO after reopening. Assertion totals vary with asynchronous waiting.

The pond-menu/developer-demo regression passes 99 checks; rendered reference/menu navigation passes 145. Final stderr logs are empty. Screenshots at 1280×720, 768×1024, 390×844 and 844×390 were inspected. These are Windows rendered/synthetic-input checks, not physical mobile-device validation. Evidence and preservation hashes are in [the experiment folder](experiments/swimmer/live-sinking-food/PROTOCOL.md); screenshots are under ignored `build/live-feeding-checks/`.

Pellets use a reduced particle model: radius 0.8 mm, density 1002 kg/m³ in 1000 kg/m³ water, linear viscous plus quadratic form drag, gravity/buoyancy, ten 5-ms substeps and a simple frictional bed/no-flux shore. Visual gold halos aid visibility. This is not CFD, pellet–pellet collision simulation or a full ingestion model. The visible simulation never autoresets; background episodes do. Longer sessions, dense edge piles, difficult bank cases and broad robustness remain follow-up work. No arms, automatic growth, rock impacts or spatial whirlpool is introduced.

Internal UI review: usability, consistency, feedback and responsive layout 9/10; hierarchy, accessibility and measured performance 8/10. Priorities are human playtesting of feeding versus pause-control emphasis, real assistive/touch checks and a joint rendering/training frame-time profile. These are review heuristics, not measured user outcomes or a reason for another cosmetic redesign now.
